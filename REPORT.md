Shark Tank India Investment Analysis – Internship Project Report
===============================================================

1. Introduction
---------------
This project was completed as part of my internship assignment. 
The goal was to analyze Shark Tank India investment data, clean the dataset, 
and create dashboards to highlight founder success patterns and funding trends.

2. Dataset
----------
- I downloaded the Shark Tank India dataset.
- The dataset contained missing values, duplicates, and inconsistent formats.
- Cleaning was required before visualization.

3. Data Cleaning (Jupyter Notebook)
-----------------------------------
- Tools used: Python and Pandas in Jupyter Notebook.
- Steps performed:
  * Handled missing values.
  * Removed duplicate records.
  * Converted datatypes for consistency.
- Output: A cleaned dataset ready for visualization.

4. Tableau Dashboards
---------------------
I created multiple sheets and dashboards in Tableau Public to visualize:
- Funding Outcomes (startups funded vs not funded).
- Investor Participation (which sharks invested most actively).
- Valuation vs Deal Amount (comparison of asked vs accepted valuations).
- Equity Trends (average equity given up by founders).
- Industry Insights (sectors attracting the most deals).

5. Founder Success Pattern Summary
----------------------------------
From the dashboard analysis, the following success patterns were observed:
- Adaptability in Valuation: Founders who adjusted their asked valuation were more likely to secure deals.
- Balanced Equity Offers: Successful founders typically offered 10–15% equity.
- Industry Focus: Food & Beverage and Technology startups attracted the highest interest.
- Investor Preferences: Sharks like Peyush Bansal and Aman Gupta invested broadly, while others focused on niche domains.
- Pitch Clarity: Clear business models and growth potential increased success rates.

6. Learning Note
----------------
During my internship, I also learned SQL basics (SELECT, JOIN, GROUP BY, AVG, importing datasets). 
However, in this project, SQL was not used. The focus was on Python (Pandas) for data cleaning 
and Tableau for visualization.

7. Conclusion
-------------
This project demonstrates how data cleaning and visualization can uncover meaningful insights 
into startup funding trends. Founder success was strongly linked to adaptability in valuation, 
balanced equity offers, and operating in scalable industries. Clear communication and alignment 
with investor preferences further increased the likelihood of funding.
